How can a commercial real estate broker find owners who may sell before they list?

Read the public filings that come before a sale: deeds, mortgages, liens, tax status, court filings and pre-foreclosure notices. Join them to each tax lot, grade owners by how many independent signals point the same way, and keep the evidence on every row.

Why public records work

Owners rarely announce a sale before they call a broker. But most of the events behind one leave a filing in a county office: a loan is recorded, a lien is placed, taxes go unpaid, a lawsuit touches the title. The records are public and dull, and there are too many for most brokerages to read.

Which records matter

  • Deeds: who owns it now, how long they’ve held it, and whether it moved between related entities.
  • Mortgages: who lent against it and, usually, when the loan comes due.
  • Liens: unpaid obligations attached to the property.
  • Tax records: whether property taxes are current.
  • Court filings: disputes that touch the property or its owner.
  • Pre-foreclosure filings: a lender has started taking it back.

How to combine them

One record is a weak signal. A lien alone can be a billing dispute, and a loan coming due is routine. Three independent records on the same lot are a different picture. Join every record to its tax lot, count the independent signals, and grade each owner by how many agree.

Check it before you trust it

Test the screen against owners you already know sold or ran into trouble. If it wouldn’t have flagged them, it won’t flag the next ones.

That test shows the screen catches situations that are already visible. Finding sales nobody has spotted yet is a harder claim, so treat the top of the list as calls worth making and track which ones convert.
