Commercial real estate brokerage

Off-market seller screen

A commercial real estate brokerage wanted to know which owners in its market might sell before a listing went up.

Finding those owners used to mean more than five hours a day of manual research in public records. The screen now does that pass across the whole market and gives the brokers a graded list, with the reason for each call.

Working out what to look for

The signals are the brokers’ own: the things they’ve seen come before a sale, like a foreclosure filing, an estate or trust on title, or an acquisition loan coming due. Each lot is graded A to D on them.

Then we had to find where each signal shows up, because the records aren’t in one place. Deeds and mortgages sit in one system. Pre-foreclosure filings sit with the county clerk. One court source blocks software altogether. Part of the job was working out which sources we could read dependably.

What we built

We loaded 2.9 million deed and mortgage records into one database and screened 51,037 tax lots against deeds, mortgages, liens, tax, housing-court and pre-foreclosure filings.

Names on a deed can mislead. For the top grade we machine-read 217 deeds, which took 15 lots off the list because the trust on title turned out to be a bank or a court referee.

Every row says whether its evidence is a deed we read, a name we classified, or a stand-in measure.
